# Crossing: Neptune / Poseidon

**The god of hidden depths — found by inferring what could not be seen.**

## The Naming

Neptune was the first planet discovered by mathematical prediction rather than observation. Urbain Le Verrier calculated its position from perturbations in Uranus's orbit in 1846; Johann Galle found it within 1° of the predicted position on the first night he looked.

The name followed the planetary convention established by Uranus: planets take Greco-Roman divine names. The colour was decisive — Neptune's deep, vivid blue made the sea god an obvious choice. The name circulated within weeks of discovery and was formally adopted with little of the controversy that had plagued the Uranus/Georgium Sidus dispute.

This is `observed`: the namers consciously chose Neptune for the blue, and the blue is a genuine physical property of the planet. Unlike the purely succession-logic of Uranus/Ouranos, here a **visual resonance** drove the naming.

## The Colour Resonance

Neptune is the bluest planet in the Solar System — a deeper, richer blue than Uranus's cyan, produced by methane absorbing red wavelengths. The colour is not metaphorical. When astronomers confirmed the planet, they saw something that looked like the sea from space before the space age.

The sea god named for his domain: a planet visually resembling the sea named after the god of the sea. The resonance is not layered or subtle — it is direct.

## The Hidden Depths Resonance

The manner of discovery adds a second layer:

**Poseidon** is the god of what lies beneath — hidden depths, submarine palaces, creatures of the deep. He rules what cannot be seen from the surface. Sailors feared him precisely because the sea's dangers were invisible until they struck.

**Neptune** was invisible — not just to the naked eye (too faint at 30 AU) but to the observational programme that found every other planet. It was not found by looking. It was found by detecting the **absence of something**: the gap between where Uranus should be and where it was. Poseidon the hidden; Neptune the inferred.

Whether the namers consciously intended this resonance is unclear — the colour was the stated reason. But the aptness is real.

## The Triton Chain

Poseidon's son **Triton** — the sea herald, half man, half fish — names Neptune's largest moon. This creates a family structure in the planetary system:

**Poseidon (Neptune)** → son **Triton** (Neptune's moon)

Mythologically, Triton was the messenger between his father's underwater realm and the surface world. Astronomically, Triton the moon is almost certainly a **captured Kuiper Belt object** — it arrived from outside the Neptunian system, pulled in from the distant cold. It is a visitor that stayed, circling in retrograde orbit, slowly spiraling inward toward eventual destruction.

The mythological Triton crossed between worlds. The astronomical Triton crossed from beyond the planetary system into Neptune's orbit. The resonance is accidental — but it is there.

## Crossing Type

`observed` — the colour resonance is real and was the primary motivation for the name. The hidden-depths layer is an additional apt resonance that the namers may not have consciously articulated.

Unlike Uranus/Ouranos (where the mythological succession logic was the explicit reason), Neptune/Poseidon is primarily a case of **seeing a blue planet and reaching for the sea god** — with a deeper aptness underneath.
