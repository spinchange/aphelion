# Jupiter (Planet)

The fifth planet from the Sun and by far the largest — a gas giant whose mass is 2.5 times the combined mass of all other planets, yet only one-thousandth the mass of the Sun. The dominant gravitational force in the Solar System after the Sun itself. Mythological counterpart: Zeus. Crossing note: [[crossing-jupiter-zeus]].

## Key Properties

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Orbital period | 11.86 years |

| Diameter | 142,984 km — 11× Earth |

## The First-Formed Planet

Jupiter was the **first planet to form** in the Solar System. Its early inward migration — the "Grand Tack" hypothesis — profoundly shaped what came after: it may have disrupted the formation of a super-Earth in the inner system (explaining why Earth exists rather than something larger) and set up the architecture of the entire planetary system.

## Gravitational Shepherd — But Not Simply Protector

Jupiter has been called the Solar System's **vacuum cleaner** because it absorbs far more asteroid and comet impacts than other planets — ~200× more than Earth. The 1994 Comet Shoemaker-Levy 9 impact demonstrated this directly.

However, 2008 computer simulations complicated the picture: Jupiter **perturbs orbits inward roughly as often as it ejects them**, meaning its net effect on Earth's impact rate may be near neutral. Some models suggest Jupiter draws long-period comets from the Oort cloud *toward* the inner system while deflecting Kuiper belt objects away. The debate continues — but Jupiter's gravitational dominance over the solar system is undeniable.

## The Galilean Moons

Discovered by Galileo Galilei in 1610 — the first proof that celestial bodies orbit something other than Earth:

- **Io** — most volcanically active body in the Solar System; sulfur dioxide geysers
- **Europa** — subsurface liquid water ocean; leading candidate for extraterrestrial life
- **Ganymede** — largest moon in the Solar System (larger than Mercury); has its own magnetic field
- **Callisto** — heavily cratered, ancient surface; possible subsurface ocean

All four orbit within Jupiter's enormous magnetosphere, the largest structure in the Solar System after the Sun's heliosphere.

## Great Red Spot

A persistent anticyclonic storm recorded since 1831, large enough to swallow Earth. It has been shrinking over the past century — from ~41,000 km wide in the 19th century to ~16,000 km today — and may eventually dissipate, though models disagree.

## Jupiter Trojans

Jupiter's gravitational dominance at its L4 and L5 Lagrange points has accumulated thousands of **Trojan asteroids** — objects orbiting in lockstep ahead and behind Jupiter. They are named after characters from the *Iliad*: the leading group (L4) after Greek heroes, the trailing group (L5) after Trojan heroes. The naming convention makes the solar system itself a mythological stage.
