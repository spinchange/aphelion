# Crossing: Sedna

**The goddess of the cold, dark, remote ocean floor — named for the cold, dark, remote outer solar system.**

This crossing introduces the fourth type in the taxonomy: `deliberate`. Unlike accidental correspondences (Mercury/Hermes) or ancient observations (Orion/Scorpius, Venus/Aphrodite), the Sedna crossing was made consciously in 2003 by the astronomers who discovered the dwarf planet. Yet it is among the most resonant in the entire cluster.

## The Deliberate Naming

Discoverers Mike Brown, Chad Trujillo, and David Rabinowitz named the object after the Inuit sea goddess [[myth-sedna|Sedna]] specifically because of the environmental correspondence: the dwarf planet exists in the **cold, dark, remote** depths of the outer solar system — Sedna's mythological domain is the **cold, dark, remote** depths of the Arctic ocean floor.

The connection is not about character traits or behaviour — it is about *place*. Sedna the goddess rules the deep cold that light barely reaches; Sedna the planet orbits in the deep cold that light barely reaches.

## The Mythological Character

In Inuit tradition, Sedna is the **origin of all marine life** — her severed fingers became the first seals, walruses, and whales. She rules from the ocean floor, controlling whether animals come to hunters' waters. She is vengeful; shamans must travel to her and comb her tangled hair (she has no fingers) to placate her and release the animals.

She is not merely a deity of place — she is a deity of **transformation at the boundary of life and death**, the moment where human violence (the finger-severing) generates the ecological abundance that makes human survival possible.

## The Astronomical Character

[[sedna-dwarf-planet|Sedna the planet]] has a perihelion of 76 AU and an aphelion of ~1,000 AU — it spends most of its 11,000-year orbit in near-total darkness, at temperatures barely above absolute zero (~12 K). During the ~200 years near perihelion, surface temperatures rise to ~35 K — enough for some ices to behave differently, but still far colder than anything in the inner solar system.

Mike Brown's description: *"I call Sedna a fossil record of the earliest Solar System."* Sedna exists at the edge, preserving in its orbit evidence of the Sun's birth cluster — a record of the Solar System's deep past, just as Sedna the goddess preserves the origin of life in her severed fingers.

## What Makes This Crossing Remarkable

All the `deliberate` crossings in this cluster [[iau-naming-conventions|(see IAU naming conventions)]] work at the surface level: an object in the outer system gets an outer/underworld deity name. But Sedna achieves something richer:

1. The environmental correspondence is vivid and specific (cold dark ocean floor / cold dark outer solar system)
2. The mythological character — transformation at the edge, the origin of life from violence — resonates with the astronomical character of a fossil record at the solar system's edge
3. The naming happened to stick in popular culture in a way most TNO names don't — "Sedna" has become known beyond astronomical circles
4. The crossing became part of earlier conversations in this very vault (the vault was nearly named after her)

## Crossing Type

`deliberate` — consciously created in 2003 by the discoverers based on environmental resonance between myth and astronomical character. This makes it categorically different from all previous crossings, but no less meaningful. The modern scientific practice of naming astronomical bodies after mythological figures is itself an ongoing, institutionalised act of astronomy-mythology crossing — and Sedna is one of its finest examples.
