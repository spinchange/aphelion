# Crossing: Pluto / Hades

**The god of the unseen — named for darkness, cold, and remoteness at the edge of the known solar system. Then demoted, as he always was.**

## The Naming

When Venetia Burney suggested "Pluto" in 1930, she was following the planetary convention — Greco-Roman deity names for planets — and noting the obvious fit: a cold, dark, distant body named after the lord of a cold, dark, distant realm. The initials PL also honoured Percival Lowell, but the mythological fit was deliberate.

The name used is the **Roman** form (*Pluto*, from *Plouton*) rather than the Greek *Hades* — consistent with the planetary convention (Jupiter, Saturn, Neptune, not Zeus, Cronus, Poseidon). *Plouton* means "the wealthy one" — the Romans favoured this euphemism for the god whose name people feared to speak.

This is `deliberate`: the naming consciously imposed the mythological correspondence. But the resonances that followed are richer than the namers could have foreseen.

## The Darkness Resonance

**Hades** rules the realm the living never see — the unseen world beneath, the final destination, the place from which no ordinary soul returns. He himself rarely leaves. He owns the dark.

**Pluto** at 30–49 AU receives roughly **0.1% of the sunlight Earth does**. At perihelion, faint sunlight; at aphelion, near-darkness. It was invisible to the naked eye for all of human history and was only found by painstaking photographic comparison. For all practical purposes, Pluto was the solar system's dark place.

The god of the unseen, named for the body no one had seen.

## The Exclusion Resonance

**Hades** was the least honoured of the three great brothers. Sometimes counted among the twelve Olympians, sometimes excluded. He had almost no active cult. He did not appear in the stories of the gods on Olympus. He was present but peripheral — the third brother who got the third domain.

**Pluto** was the ninth planet for 76 years, then **demoted** to dwarf planet in 2006 — reclassified out of the canonical list. Not a full planet; not quite in the club. The body named for the god who was never quite counted among the Olympians was itself eventually not quite counted among the planets.

Whether this is coincidence or pattern depends on how you read mythology. But the parallel is precise.

## The Moon System — The Most Mythologised Body

What makes this crossing exceptional is the satellite system. Every one of Pluto's five moons is named after an inhabitant of Hades's realm:

| Moon | Mythological figure | Role in the underworld |
|---|---|---|
| **Charon** | The ferryman | Carries souls across the River Styx |
| **Styx** | The river | Boundary between living and dead; the gods' unbreakable oath |
| **Nix** | Nyx, goddess of night | Mother of Charon; personification of darkness |
| **Kerberos** | Cerberus | The three-headed guard dog; no soul escapes |
| **Hydra** | The serpent | Nine-headed guardian of the underworld's entrance |

No other body in the solar system has its entire satellite system named as a coherent mythological household. Pluto orbits with its underworld entourage intact: the ferryman, the river, the night, the guard, the monster.

**Charon** is especially significant: at half Pluto's diameter, the two form a true binary system, orbiting a barycentre above Pluto's surface. Mythologically, Charon is the permanent intermediary between the realm of the dead and the world above — a body perpetually in relation to Pluto, never leaving.

## Crossing Type

`deliberate` — the naming was a conscious mythological choice. The depth of the resonance (darkness, exclusion, demotion, the moon system) goes far beyond the original intent and makes this the most extensively mythologised body in the solar system.
