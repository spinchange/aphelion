# Sirius

The brightest star in the night sky (magnitude −1.46) and one of the Sun's nearest neighbours at ~8.6 light-years. The Dog Star of Canis Major — Orion's hunting dog in the [[orion-constellation|constellation]] mythology. The star around which the ancient Egyptian calendar was organised. The Polynesian latitude marker for Fiji. Referenced in the Qur'an. Probably the most culturally significant star in human history after the [[polaris|North Star]] and the [[pleiades-cluster|Pleiades]].

## Physical Properties

A binary system: **Sirius A** (main sequence, ~2× solar mass) and **Sirius B** (white dwarf, ~1 solar mass in Earth-sized volume — first white dwarf identified). Sirius A is so luminous because it is both intrinsically bright and nearby. Sirius B was the first white dwarf ever identified, completing its main-sequence life about 120 million years ago.

Sirius's heliacal rising — its first appearance above the eastern horizon before sunrise after a 70-day absence — is **remarkably precise**: almost exactly 365.25 days, holding constant relative to the solar year due to its unusual position relative to the ecliptic.

## Egypt — The Calendar Star

The heliacal rising of Sirius at Cairo (July 19, Julian calendar) heralded the **annual flooding of the Nile** in antiquity. Because the flood's own irregularity made precise prediction vital, the extreme regularity of Sirius's return made it indispensable.

The Egyptians worshipped Sirius as the goddess **Sopdet** (Greek: Sothis) — "Triangle," guarantor of fertility. Orion was worshipped as **Sah**, her husband. Sopdu, their sky-god son, completed the divine family. Sopdet was later syncretised with **Isis**; Sah was linked with **Osiris** — so Homer could truthfully write that "the soul of Isis is called Dog by the Greeks."

The **70-day absence** of Sirius from the sky was understood as Sopdet-Isis and Sah-Osiris passing through the Egyptian underworld (*Duat*) — a mythological encoding of the star's actual invisibility period in conjunction with the Sun.

The Sothic cycle (~1,460 years) — the time for the heliacal rising to precess through the Egyptian civil calendar — was used to anchor Egyptian chronology.

## Greece — The Dog Days

The Greeks feared Sirius's return. Its appearance as the morning star heralded the hottest, most oppressive days of summer. They believed its "emanations" caused plants to wilt, men to weaken, and women to become aroused. Anyone suffering these effects was *astrobólētos* — "star-struck."

The summer period following Sirius's return became the **"dog days"** (*dies caniculares* in Latin) — the hottest weeks of the year. Homer in the *Iliad*: "Sirius rises late in the dark, liquid sky / On summer nights, star of stars, Orion's Dog they call it, brightest / Of all, but an evil portent, bringing heat / And fevers to suffering humanity."

## Polynesian Navigation

Sirius was a critical navigational star for Polynesian seafarers in the Pacific. The Polynesians constructed a "Great Bird" constellation:
- **Sirius** = body of the bird (*Manu*)
- **[[canopus|Canopus]]** = southern wingtip
- **Procyon** = northern wingtip

This divided the Pacific night sky into two hemispheres. Sirius's declination (~−17°) passes **directly overhead at Fiji's latitude** (17°S), making it a precision latitude marker for Fijian waters.

For the Māori, Sirius was *Takurua* — both the name of the star and of the winter season. Its winter solstice culmination was celebrated in Hawaii as "Queen of Heaven."

## Cross-Cultural Dog Associations

The dog association for Sirius appears across unconnected cultures:
- Greek/Roman: Dog Star (Canis Major = Great Dog = Orion's dog)
- Chinese: "celestial wolf"
- Various Indigenous North American: Seri and Tohono O'odham (dog that follows mountain sheep), Blackfoot ("Dog-face"), Cherokee (paired with Antares as dog-star guardian of the "Path of Souls"), Pawnee ("Wolf Star"), Inuit ("Moon Dog")

## The Qur'an

Sirius is mentioned in Surah An-Najm (53:49): "And that He is the Lord of Sirius (*ash-shi'rā*)." Some pre-Islamic Arab tribes worshipped Sirius; the verse asserts Allah as its lord.
