# Neptune (Planet)

The eighth and farthest planet from the Sun — the only planet in the Solar System found by mathematical prediction before it was seen through a telescope. An ice giant of deep methane blue, with the strongest winds in the Solar System and a moon destined to be torn apart. Mythological counterpart: [[myth-poseidon]]. Crossing note: [[crossing-neptune-poseidon]].

## The Mathematical Discovery

Neptune was not found by scanning the sky — it was found by calculation. By the 1840s, Uranus's orbit had accumulated small but persistent deviations from Newtonian predictions. Two mathematicians independently computed where an unknown planet must be to produce those perturbations:

- **Urbain Le Verrier** (France) and **John Couch Adams** (Britain) both solved the problem
- Le Verrier's prediction was used first: he sent coordinates to Johann Gottfried Galle in Berlin
- Galle found Neptune **within 1° of the predicted position** on September 23, 1846 — on the first night he looked

Neptune completed its first full orbit since discovery in **July 2011** — the planet had been known for less than one Neptunian year.

## The Blue Planet

Neptune's deep blue colour — richer and more vivid than Uranus's cyan — comes from methane absorbing red light. An additional unknown chromophore likely deepens the blue beyond what methane alone produces. The colour is not incidental to the name: when the planet was confirmed, its striking blue made Neptune — god of the sea — an obvious choice.

Unlike Uranus, Neptune radiates **2.6× more heat than it receives from the Sun**, suggesting significant internal heat generation. Despite being farther from the Sun, Neptune's atmosphere is livelier than Uranus's: its Great Dark Spot (observed by Voyager 2 in 1989) was a storm the size of Earth. Hubble showed it had vanished by 1994 and new spots have appeared since, making Neptunian storms transient in a way Jovian ones are not.

## Triton

Neptune's largest moon is one of the strangest objects in the Solar System:

- **Retrograde orbit** — orbits in the opposite direction to Neptune's rotation, the only large moon in the Solar System to do so
- Almost certainly a **captured Kuiper Belt object** — pulled in from beyond Neptune, it never formed in place
- Surface temperature: **−235°C** — one of the coldest measured surfaces in the Solar System
- Active **nitrogen geysers** — dark plumes shoot ~8 km into the thin atmosphere
- Slowly **spiraling inward** due to tidal friction: in approximately 3.6 billion years, tidal forces will shred Triton apart, creating a ring system that will rival Saturn's

The name Triton is mythologically apt: Triton was the son of Poseidon and Amphitrite — Neptune's heir, circling its parent. See [[myth-poseidon]].

## Rings

Neptune's five ring arcs are faint and clumpy — particles concentrate in arc segments rather than continuous bands, held in place by the shepherd moon Galatea. The Adams ring contains three named arcs: Liberté, Égalité, Fraternité.

## The Winds

Neptune's 2,100 km/h winds are the fastest measured on any planet. Their energy source is unknown: Neptune receives very little solar energy at 30 AU, yet drives storms more intense than any other planet. The internal heat excess likely drives the atmospheric dynamics, but the mechanism is unresolved.
