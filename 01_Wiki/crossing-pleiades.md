# Crossing: The Pleiades

**The most globally entangled mythology-astronomy correspondence in human history.**

Unlike the planetary crossings, the Pleiades crossing is not between a god and a wandering planet — it is between a cluster of nymphs and a cluster of stars, where the mythology and astronomy have been **inseparable for at least 3,600 years** and possibly 100,000.

## The Core Correspondence

The [[pleiades-cluster]] is an open star cluster ~444 light-years away in Taurus. It contains over 1,000 stars; 6–7 are visible to the naked eye. The [[myth-pleiades|mythological Pleiades]] are seven daughters of Atlas, companions of Artemis, pursued eternally by Orion.

The crossing operates on multiple levels simultaneously:

**The Number Seven** — Every culture names seven sisters, even though only six are typically visible. The mythological explanation (the lost Pleiad) maps directly to the astronomical observation of a "missing" seventh. This may be genuine folk-astronomy — encoding a real astronomical puzzle in a memorable narrative.

**The Pursuit** — Orion (winter constellation) eternally pursues the Pleiades (associated with spring). This is [[crossing-orion-scorpius|astronomically real]]: when the Pleiades rise at dusk in autumn, Orion has set; when the Pleiades set in spring, Orion rises. The myth encodes the actual sky mechanics of a chase that has no resolution.

**The Calendar** — The Pleiades' heliacal rising marked the start of the Mediterranean sailing and planting season. Hesiod's *Works and Days* organises agricultural activity around their risings and settings. The myth of the sisters (*plein* = to sail) encodes practical astronomical knowledge as narrative.

**The Genealogy** — Maia, eldest of the Pleiades, is **Hermes's mother**. The mythological lineage connects the Pleiades directly to [[myth-hermes|Hermes]] and [[crossing-mercury-hermes|the Mercury crossing]] — making the Pleiades the grandmother-cluster of the entire mythology × astronomy web we've built. Orion pursues them; Hermes is their grandchild.

## The Lost Pleiad as Astronomy

Only six Pleiades are easily visible naked-eye. The universal myth of a missing seventh — found in Greek, Indigenous Australian, Japanese, and multiple other independent traditions — may encode an actual astronomical observation from **~100,000 years ago**, when Pleione was separated from Atlas and genuinely visible as a seventh distinct star. If so, this is the oldest continuous astronomical tradition in human knowledge.

## The Nebra Sky Disk

The Nebra sky disk (~1600 BC, Germany) shows seven dots alongside the Sun and Moon — the **earliest known material depiction of the Pleiades**, confirming the identification at least 3,600 years ago, long before Greek civilisation.

In Babylonian astronomy, MULMUL ("star star") heads the list of ecliptic stars — because in 2330 BC the Pleiades marked the vernal equinox. They were literally the starting point of the celestial calendar.

## Crossing Type

`observed` — the most deeply, deliberately observed crossing in the set. The Pleiades' agricultural and navigational significance made precise tracking essential for survival. The mythology is not merely decorative; it is a **mnemonic system for astronomical knowledge**, encoding seasonal markers, the duration of Orion's retreat, the sailors' window, and the calendar's structure in a form that could be transmitted across thousands of years without writing.
