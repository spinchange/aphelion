# Pleiades (Mythology)

The seven daughter-nymphs of the Titan Atlas and the Oceanid Pleione — companions of Artemis, placed in the stars after Zeus transformed them to escape Orion's pursuit. The most globally recognised star group in human cultural history. Astronomical counterpart: [[pleiades-cluster]]. Crossing note: [[crossing-pleiades]].

## The Seven Sisters

| Name | Notable children | Father |
|---|---|---|
| **Maia** (eldest) | **Hermes** | Zeus |
| Electra | Dardanus (ancestor of Troy) | Zeus |
| Taygete | Lacedaemon (founder of Sparta) | Zeus |
| Alcyone | Hyrieus | Poseidon |
| Celaeno | Lycus, Nycteus | Poseidon |
| Sterope/Asterope | Oenomaus | Ares |
| **Merope** (youngest) | Glaucus | Sisyphus (mortal) |

**Maia** is the key figure connecting the Pleiades to the existing mythology × astronomy cluster. She is **Hermes's mother** — making the Pleiades the grandmother-cluster of the whole web: Orion pursues the Pleiades, Hermes is the son of Maia (a Pleiad), and Orion's mythology is already captured in [[crossing-orion-scorpius]] and [[myth-orion]].

## The Eternal Pursuit

Orion, the supreme hunter, pursued the Pleiades (or their mother Pleione) across the Earth for **seven years**. Zeus intervened, transforming them first to doves and then to stars. But the constellation of Orion still faces the direction of the Pleiades cluster — the pursuit continues across the sky for eternity.

Hesiod in *Works and Days*: "when the Pleiades flee mighty Orion and plunge into the misty deep... it is time to plough." The sisters mark the agricultural calendar precisely because Orion — winter — rises as they set; they — spring — rise as Orion sets.

## The Lost Pleiad

Only six Pleiades are easily visible to the naked eye, though seven are named. The "lost Pleiad" was explained mythologically:
- **Merope** hides in shame because she married a mortal (Sisyphus) rather than a god — the only sister to do so
- **Electra** veiled herself in grief when Troy fell — she was the ancestral mother of the Trojans via Dardanus

Some scientists suggest the seven-sisters motif across cultures (found in Greek, Indigenous Australian, Japanese, and many other traditions independently) may date to **100,000+ years ago**, when the star Pleione was separated enough from Atlas to be visible separately, giving a true seven-star naked-eye cluster.

## Agricultural and Navigational Calendar

The name *Pleiades* likely derives from *plein* (to sail) rather than mythology — the heliacal rising of the Pleiades in May marked the start of Mediterranean sailing season; their setting in November marked its end. Hesiod's calendar is explicitly organised around their rising and setting. The **name of the star cluster gave its name to the mythological sisters**, not the other way around.
