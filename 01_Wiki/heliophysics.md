# Heliophysics

The scientific study of the Sun and its influence throughout the solar system — encompassing the Sun's behavior, the [[interplanetary-medium|solar wind]], [[space-weather]], Earth's [[geospace|magnetosphere]], and the boundaries of the [[heliosphere]]. NASA's Heliophysics Division pursues this as a unified system science: the Sun, the solar wind, and every planetary space environment it touches are studied as one interconnected system.

## Four Focus Areas

**[[solar-science|Solar Science]]** — The Sun as a dynamic plasma star. Magnetic field cycles driving the 11-year activity cycle; solar flares, coronal mass ejections, and solar energetic particles; the corona and solar wind origin. Key missions: Parker Solar Probe, SDO, SOHO, Solar Orbiter.

**[[space-weather|Space Weather]]** — The effects of solar activity on the near-Earth environment and human technology. CME-driven geomagnetic storms; radio blackouts; GPS disruption; satellite damage; power grid impacts. Forecasting from the L1 Lagrange point via DSCOVR.

**Magnetospheres** — Earth's [[geospace|magnetosphere]] is a vast comet-shaped bubble generated by the convective motion of molten iron in Earth's outer core. It shields the planet from solar wind erosion and cosmic particle radiation. Its dayside extends 6–10 Earth radii; its magnetotail fluctuates to hundreds of Earth radii. NASA also studies magnetospheres of other planets (Jupiter via Juno, etc.). Key missions: MMS, THEMIS, Van Allen Probes, BARREL.

**Ionosphere, Thermosphere, and Mesosphere (ITM)** — The thin shell 50–400 miles above Earth where the neutral atmosphere transitions into ionized plasma. The ionosphere is sensitive to solar activity and responds dramatically to [[space-weather]] events. It is both home to low Earth-orbiting spacecraft and the medium through which radio communications travel. Energy is not just deposited here — it is transformed and transported, making the ITM an active source of plasma into the magnetosphere rather than a passive boundary.

## Why It Matters

- **Habitability** — The Sun's radiation is both essential to life and hazardous in excess. Understanding its variability informs Earth's long-term climate context.
- **Space technology** — [[space-weather]] can damage satellites, disrupt GPS and radio, and threaten astronauts. Better heliophysics science improves forecasting and protection.
- **Universal physics** — Earth's space environment is the only place in the universe where complex electromagnetic plasma physics can be studied in situ. Findings generalize to stellar physics throughout the cosmos.
- **Exploration** — Future crewed missions to the Moon and Mars require robust space weather prediction to protect astronauts beyond Earth's magnetospheric shield.
