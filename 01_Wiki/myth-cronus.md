# Cronus (Mythology)

Leader of the Titans — the generation of gods before the Olympians — who ruled during the mythological **Golden Age**. His Roman counterpart is **Saturn**. Planet correspondence: [[saturn-planet]]. Crossing note: [[crossing-saturn-cronus]].

## The Golden Age

Cronus's reign was called the Golden Age: a time when "people had no need for laws or rules; everyone did the right thing, and immorality was absent." This pre-Olympian paradise — before Zeus, before law, before human struggle — is the mythological past that Saturn encodes. The harvest festival Kronia was held in his honour, during which social hierarchies were temporarily suspended and masters served their slaves — echoing the Golden Age's equality.

## The Castration and Rise

Cronus overthrew his father Uranus using a stone sickle given to him by Gaia. The severed genitals fell into the sea — producing the sea foam from which Aphrodite was born (the first act of cosmic creation after the first act of cosmic violence). Cronus then married his sister Rhea and ruled the cosmos.

## Swallowing His Children

Warned by prophecy that his own children would overthrow him — just as he had overthrown Uranus — Cronus swallowed each child as Rhea bore them: Hestia, Hades, Demeter, Poseidon, Hera. When the sixth child Zeus was born, Rhea substituted a stone wrapped in swaddling clothes. Cronus swallowed the stone.

One version adds: when Rhea pressed her breast to the stone-baby, the milk that sprayed across the heavens **created the Milky Way**. The galaxy as a consequence of Cronus's failure of perception.

## Fall and Aftermath

Zeus freed his siblings, overthrew Cronus and the Titans, and imprisoned most in Tartarus. Cronus's fate varies by source — some have him imprisoned forever; others have Zeus eventually release him to rule the **Isles of the Blessed**, where heroes dwell in the afterlife. Pindar: "Zeus freed the Titans."

## Symbols

The sickle, the scythe, grain — originally a **harvest deity** whose month Cronion was named after him. The association with time (Chronos) developed later through folk etymology, but it became so dominant that for centuries Saturn-as-Time was the primary iconographic reading.
