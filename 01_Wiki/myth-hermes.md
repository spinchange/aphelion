# Hermes (Mythology)

Olympian deity of ancient Greek religion — the herald of the gods, messenger, psychopomp, and god of boundaries, roads, travelers, merchants, thieves, and speed. His Roman counterpart is Mercury, whose name derives from Latin *merx* ("merchandise"), origin of "merchant" and "commerce."

Astronomical correspondence: [[mercury-planet]]. Crossing note: [[crossing-mercury-hermes]].

## Core Identity

Hermes is above all a god of **transitions and thresholds** — between realms, between states, between people. His roles all converge on this:

- **Messenger of the gods** — conveys messages between Olympus, the mortal world, and the Underworld; the only Olympian who could cross all three without restriction
- **Psychopomp** — guides souls of the deceased to the afterlife; his image appeared commonly on Greek gravestones
- **God of boundaries** — his name derives from *herma* (stone pile); travelers historically added stones to boundary cairns sacred to him
- **Liminal mediator** — "due to his mobility and his liminal nature, mediating between opposites," he was considered the divine principle of connection and accessibility

Hermes is described as the **youngest Olympian** — in some myths his early role involved negotiating with the older celestial gods to enter the pantheon.

## Attributes

**Winged sandals** (*talaria*) — "able to take the roads with the speed of wind"; in later art, wings spring directly from the ankles
**Caduceus** — staff with two intertwined serpents, sometimes crowned with wings; documented among Babylonians (~3500 BC) and linked to the Mesopotamian god Ningishzida, who also served as mediator between humans and the divine
**Petasos** — winged helmet or traveller's hat
**Satchel** — for carrying messages across any distance

## Ancient Origins

The earliest written record is in Linear B (Mycenaean Greek) from Pylos, Thebes, and Knossos — Bronze Age, predating Homer by centuries. His earliest form was almost certainly **chthonic** (underworld-associated), probably related to boundaries between life and death. The messenger role developed from the psychopomp role: one who can cross the boundary between realms is naturally the right agent to carry messages between them.

Homer's *Iliad* calls him "the bringer of good luck, guide and guardian, excellent in all the tricks." Hesiod's *Works and Days* has him giving Pandora the gifts of lies and seductive words.

## Cross-Cultural Parallels

The messenger-god archetype appears **independently** across cultures, each associating it with the same planet:
- **Babylonian Nabu** — messenger to the gods, associated with the planet Mercury (~1st millennium BC)
- **Egyptian Thoth** — divine scribe and messenger; Herodotus first identified him with Hermes
- **Norse Odin/Woden** — associated with Mercury (hence *Mercredi/Wednesday* = "Mercury's day" / "Woden's day")
- **Maya** — Mercury as messenger owl to the underworld
- **Hindu Budha** — rules Wednesday

The convergence is not coincidence — see [[crossing-mercury-hermes]].
