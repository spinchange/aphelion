# Orion (Constellation)

One of the most prominent constellations in the sky — a winter constellation in the Northern Hemisphere, summer in the Southern. Named after [[myth-orion|Orion the Hunter]]. Crossing note: [[crossing-orion-scorpius]].

## Key Stars

**Rigel** (β Ori, magnitude 0.12) — blue-white supergiant, one of the brightest stars in the sky; represents Orion's left knee
**Betelgeuse** (α Ori) — red supergiant, slightly variable; represents his right shoulder. A future [[supernova]] candidate — when it explodes, it will briefly be visible in daylight.
**Orion's Belt** — three stars in a short straight line: Mintaka, Alnilam, Alnitak

## The Orion Nebula

Hanging from Orion's Belt is the **Orion Nebula** (M42) — one of the brightest nebulae visible to the naked eye, appearing as a fuzzy star in the sword. It is:
- ~1,500 light-years from Earth
- An active stellar nursery containing the **Trapezium cluster** — newborn stars only hundreds of thousands of years old, including brown dwarfs
- Part of the **Orion Molecular Cloud Complex** — one of the most intense regions of stellar formation visible from Earth, hundreds of light-years across

The complex forms the eastern part of the **Orion-Eridanus Superbubble**, a structure visible in X-rays and hydrogen emission — a vast cavity carved by past star formation, sharing topology with the [[local-bubble]] our Solar System inhabits.

The Orion-Eridanus Superbubble and the local stellar associations (including those in Ophiuchus and Taurus) are the molecular clouds lying along the borders of the [[local-bubble]], mentioned in the `interstellar-medium` note.

## Visibility and Seasonality

Orion is most visible in the evening sky from **January to April** in the Northern Hemisphere. In summer (May–July), it is in the daytime sky — invisible. This seasonal absence is the astronomical fact encoded in the Orion/Scorpius myth:

- **Scorpius** (Scorpio) is a summer constellation — dominant in July-August skies
- As Scorpius rises in the east at summer dusk, Orion sets in the west
- As Orion rises in the east at winter dusk, Scorpius sets below the western horizon
- The two constellations **never appear together** in the night sky

The myth says Zeus placed them on opposite sides to keep them apart after Scorpius killed Orion. The sky mechanics match perfectly — see [[crossing-orion-scorpius]].

## The Orionids

The **Orionid meteor shower** peaks around October 20 each year, appearing to radiate from the Orion constellation border with Gemini. Its parent body: **Halley's Comet**. Up to ~20 meteors per hour at peak.

## Precession Note

Orion currently straddles the celestial equator, visible from almost all of Earth. Due to precession of Earth's axis, by AD 14000 it will have drifted far enough south to no longer be visible from the latitude of Britain.
