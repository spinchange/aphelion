# Aphrodite (Mythology)

Ancient Greek goddess of love, lust, beauty, pleasure, and desire. Her Roman counterpart is **Venus** (from *merx*, "merchandise"), whose name gives the planet [[venus-planet]] its identity. Crossing note: [[crossing-venus-aphrodite]].

Her cult was largely derived from the Phoenician goddess **Astarte**, herself cognate with the Sumerian **Inanna/Ishtar** — making Aphrodite the western end of a lineage stretching back to the earliest urban civilisations.

## Two Origins, Two Aphrodites

Hesiod's *Theogony* gives the older tradition: Aphrodite rose from the **sea foam** (*aphros*) produced when Cronus castrated Uranus and cast his severed genitals into the sea. She emerged near Cythera or Cyprus — both claimed to be her birthplace.

Homer's *Iliad* gives a different account: daughter of Zeus and the Titaness Dione.

Plato distinguished these as two separate entities: **Aphrodite Ourania** (Heavenly — older, born from the sea, inspiring spiritual love) and **Aphrodite Pandemos** (Common — daughter of Zeus, inspiring earthly desire). This distinction shaped centuries of art and philosophy.

## Character

Married to Hephaestus (the divine smith), but perpetually unfaithful — most famously with Ares, a liaison caught by Hephaestus's bronze net. This pairing is cosmically noted: Ares's children Phobos and Deimos (Fear and Dread) exist alongside Aphrodite's children Eros and Harmonia — war and love intertwined.

Epithets include *Philommeides* ("smile-loving"), *Philommēdēs* ("laughter-loving"), and the warlike *Areia* — used specifically in Sparta and Crete where she was worshipped as a warrior goddess.

Symbols: doves, sparrows, swans, sea-foam, roses, myrtle, conch shells. Doves may derive from Inanna's sacred bird *peristerá* — a Semitic phrase meaning "bird of Ishtar."

## Inanna Parallel

The deepest connection to the planet Venus (see [[crossing-venus-aphrodite]]) may predate the Greeks entirely. The Sumerian goddess **Inanna**, whose mythology likely fed into Aphrodite via Ishtar and Astarte, makes **movements in her myths that directly parallel Venus's movement in the sky**. In *Inanna's Descent to the Underworld*, she descends and returns — just as Venus disappears in the west and rises again in the east. Theology professor Jeffrey Cooley has argued these movements are intentional astronomical encodings.
