# Mercury (Planet)

The innermost planet of the Solar System — smallest, fastest, and most eccentric of the eight planets. The astronomical body paired with the mythological [[myth-hermes]]. The crossing note documenting their remarkable correspondence is [[crossing-mercury-hermes]].

## Key Orbital Properties

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Orbital period | 87.969 Earth days |
| Synodic period | 116 Earth days (inferior conjunction cycle) |
| Max elongation from Sun | 17.9° (perihelion) to 27.8° (aphelion) |
| Orbital eccentricity | 0.21 — most eccentric of all planets |
| Distance from Sun | 46–70 million km |
| Orbital speed | ~47 km/s (fastest planet) |

## The Proximity Paradox

Mercury is the **closest planet, on average, to every other planet in the Solar System** — including Earth, Mars, Jupiter, and all others.

This is deeply counterintuitive. Venus has a closer orbit to Earth's and can approach within ~38 million km at inferior conjunction, while Mercury's closest approach is ~82 million km. Yet Venus spends much of its time on the far side of the Sun, enormously distant. Mercury, never straying more than ~28° from the Sun, is never very far from anything.

The mathematical result: when time-averaged, Mercury's mean distance to any given planet is less than the mean distance of any other planet to that planet. Formalized in: Stockman, Monroe & Cordner, "Venus is not Earth's closest neighbor," *Physics Today*, 2019.

Wikipedia's own note on this: *"Essentially, because Mercury is closest to the Sun, when taking an average over time, Mercury is most often the closest planet to the Earth, and — in that measure — it is the closest planet to each of the other planets in the Solar System."*

## Ancient Observation

Mercury is difficult to observe — always within ~28° of the Sun, visible only briefly at dawn or dusk and lost in solar glare. Despite this, ancient astronomers tracked it with remarkable precision:

- **~14th century BC (Assyrian):** MUL.APIN tablets name Mercury "the jumping planet" — capturing its erratic horizon appearances
- **~1st millennium BC (Babylonian):** Systematic records; Mercury called **Nabu** — their messenger to the gods — an independent naming that mirrors Hermes (see [[crossing-mercury-hermes]])
- **Ancient China:** "Hour Star" / "Water Star"
- **Maya:** messenger owl to the underworld
- **Norse:** associated with Odin/Woden (hence "Wednesday" = Mercury's day)

The cross-cultural convergence on the **messenger** archetype — independently, across civilizations that had no contact — is the heart of the [[crossing-mercury-hermes]] note.

## Physical Character

Mercury's extreme orbital eccentricity (0.21) combined with its 3:2 spin-orbit resonance (rotates 3 times for every 2 orbits) creates bizarre surface conditions: an observer at certain points would watch the Sun rise, partially reverse, set, then rise again — all within a single Mercurian day. The Sun appears to go retrograde near perihelion.

Mercury's perihelion precession (43 arcseconds per century unexplained by Newtonian mechanics) was one of the key tests that Einstein's general relativity passed in 1915.

The [[parker-solar-probe]] approaches Mercury multiple times to use gravitational assists on its way to the Sun — the innermost planet as a stepping stone to understand the [[solar-corona]].
