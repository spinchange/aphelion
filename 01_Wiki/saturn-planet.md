# Saturn (Planet)

The sixth planet from the Sun and the second largest — a gas giant famous for its spectacular ring system, the most extensive planetary ring system in the Solar System. Slowest of the five naked-eye planets. Mythological counterpart: [[myth-cronus]]. Crossing note: [[crossing-saturn-cronus]].

## Key Properties

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Orbital period | **29.46 years** — slowest naked-eye planet |
| Distance from Sun | 9.58 AU average |
| Diameter | ~120,536 km (~9.5 Earths) |
| Mass | ~95 Earth masses |
| Density | 0.69 g/cm³ — less dense than water; would float |
| Moons | 146 confirmed (most in Solar System) |
| Rotation | ~10.7 hours (fastest rotation visible in size/shape) |
| Ring system | Extends ~282,000 km from planet; ~20 m thick |

## The Rings

Saturn's rings are composed primarily of ice particles, rocky debris, and dust — ranging from micrometres to metres in size. Galileo first observed them in 1610 but couldn't understand them; he described two "companions." Christiaan Huygens correctly identified them as a disc in 1655. The rings have been divided into named groups: A, B, C rings (visible), D, E, F, G (fainter).

The rings are not ancient — current models suggest they formed 100–400 million years ago, possibly from a shattered moon or comet. They are actively losing mass to Saturn at a rate that will deplete them within ~100 million years.

## The 29-Year Cycle

Saturn's ~29.46-year orbit means it takes nearly **three decades** to complete one circuit of the sky — by far the slowest of the five classical planets visible to the naked eye. In a human lifetime, most people see Saturn complete only two or three full circuits. Ancient astronomers found this reliable slowness cosmically significant: the planet that moved least, changed least, was associated with what endures, what preceded, what rules from before — Cronus, the Titan who came before Zeus.

## Titan

Saturn's largest moon is **Titan** — the only moon in the Solar System with a thick atmosphere (denser than Earth's). Titan has liquid methane lakes and rivers, methane rain, and a complex organic chemistry. The Cassini-Huygens mission (2004–2017) revealed a world with a complete methane hydrological cycle analogous to Earth's water cycle. Titan is a candidate location for life as we don't know it.

## Moons as Mythology

Saturn's moons are named after Titans and their associates: Titan, Rhea, Dione, Tethys, Enceladus, Hyperion, Iapetus, Phoebe, Mimas — the generation of gods that preceded the Olympians, just as Cronus/Saturn preceded Zeus/Jupiter. Even the naming conventions encode the mythology.
