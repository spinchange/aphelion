# Outer Space Treaty

The foundational multilateral treaty of international space law. Full name: *Treaty on Principles Governing the Activities of States in the Exploration and Use of Outer Space, including the Moon and Other Celestial Bodies*. Signed 27 January 1967; entered into force 10 October 1967. As of October 2025, 118 state parties have ratified or acceded; 20 more are signatories. All major spacefaring nations are parties.

Referenced in [[outer-space]] as the framework establishing that space is not subject to national sovereignty.

## Core Principles

Per the UN Office for Outer Space Affairs:

- Exploration and use of outer space shall be for the benefit and in the interests of all countries — outer space is "the province of all mankind"
- Outer space is free for exploration and use by all states
- Outer space is not subject to national appropriation by sovereignty, use, occupation, or any other means
- States shall not place nuclear weapons or weapons of mass destruction in orbit, on celestial bodies, or elsewhere in space
- The Moon and other celestial bodies shall be used exclusively for peaceful purposes — no weapons testing, military maneuvers, or military bases
- Astronauts shall be regarded as envoys of mankind
- States are responsible and liable for national space activities, whether governmental or private
- States shall avoid harmful contamination of space and celestial bodies

## What It Permits

The treaty does **not** prohibit conventional weapons in space, military personnel supporting peaceful activities, or military space forces. Anti-satellite weapons (ASAT) tests by the US, USSR, China, and India (2019) remain legal under the treaty, though they create hazardous [[space-debris]].

## Legal Lineage

Drew heavily from the Antarctic Treaty (1961). The OST was the cornerstone of a network of Cold War space law:

| Treaty | Year | Subject |
|---|---|---|
| Rescue Agreement | 1968 | Safe return of astronauts |
| Space Liability Convention | 1972 | Liability for spacecraft damage |
| Registration Convention | 1976 | Registration of space objects |
| Moon Treaty | 1979 | Jurisdiction over celestial bodies |

The Moon Treaty has only 18 parties — no spacefaring nation has ratified it.

## Gaps and Tensions

Silent or ambiguous on: asteroid and lunar resource mining, commercial space activities, [[space-debris]] liability, and military activities short of WMD. These gaps are increasingly contested as commercial spaceflight expands.
