# Crossing: Mercury Retrograde — A False Crossing

**Mercury retrograde disrupts communication and travel. Ancient navigators would have been unaffected — because Mercury was useless for navigation.**

This note introduces a fifth crossing type: `false`. A correspondence asserted in popular culture that does not hold in astronomical practice. Understanding why it's false deepens the understanding of both the mythology and the astronomy.

## The Modern Claim

In contemporary astrology, Mercury retrograde (occurring ~three times per year for ~three weeks) is blamed for: communication breakdowns, travel delays, technology failures, missed messages, and general chaos in affairs governed by Mercury/Hermes's domain.

The logic is deductive from mythology: Hermes governs communication and travel. When his planet goes backward, communication and travel go backward.

## Why Ancient Navigators Would Not Have Cared

[[celestial-navigation]] relies on **fixed stars** — objects that rise and set at the same point on the horizon every night (with seasonal drift predictable by simple observation). A star's rising azimuth gives you a compass bearing. Its altitude at transit gives you your latitude.

The planets, by definition, *wander* — the Greek word *planetes* means "wanderer." A wanderer cannot give you a consistent bearing. Ancient navigators knew this and used planets only as clock-checks or supplementary reference, not for primary navigation.

**Mercury in particular was worse than useless for navigation:**
- Max elongation from the Sun: ~28°
- Visible only in dawn or dusk twilight, close to the horizon
- The horizon at twilight is the worst position for star navigation: atmospheric refraction is greatest, the horizon reference is ambiguous, and the sky is bright enough to wash out stars
- Mercury's retrograde period (~3 weeks every 116 days) would occur almost entirely while Mercury was too close to the Sun to observe anyway

The Polynesian navigators who crossed 2,000+ miles of open Pacific memorised ~150 stars. Mercury was not among their navigational stars. The Arab astronomers who named most of the sky's bright stars had no navigational use for Mercury. The Norse, using the sunstone to find the Sun's position through overcast, had no use for Mercury. Harrison's chronometer solved longitude — Mercury was irrelevant.

## What the Babylonians Actually Tracked

The [[babylonian-astronomy|Babylonians]], who tracked Mercury from at least the 14th century BC and called it Nabu (messenger to the gods), tracked it as an **omen indicator**, not as a navigation reference. The *Enuma Anu Enlil* interpreted Mercury's heliacal risings and settings as omens — but these were read for political and agricultural forecasting, not for steering ships.

The Babylonian navigators who *did* use stars for navigation used fixed stars — Pleiades, Sirius, Scorpius — not the wandering planets.

## The Astrological Logic Is Internal

The Mercury retrograde harm is real within the astrological system — it follows coherently from the premise that planetary characters govern their domains. But it is **derived from mythological character**, not from observational astronomy. No ancient navigator, farmer, or traveller in the Bronze Age would have modified their behaviour based on Mercury's retrograde phase, because they were not watching Mercury closely enough to know when it was retrograde.

The contrast with [[crossing-orion-scorpius]] is instructive: that crossing was directly observed (Orion and Scorpius visibly never share the sky) and became mythology. Mercury retrograde runs in reverse — mythology became astrological practice without the observational grounding.

## A New Crossing Type

`false` — a claimed correspondence between mythology and astronomy that does not hold in actual astronomical practice. The mythological logic is internally consistent but disconnected from the physical reality of the sky.

This is distinct from `accidental` (Mercury/Hermes — a real correspondence the ancients couldn't have derived) or `deliberate` (Sedna — a conscious modern naming). It is mythology extending beyond what the sky can support.
