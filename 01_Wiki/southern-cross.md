# Southern Cross (Crux)

The smallest constellation in the sky (68.4 square degrees) and the most culturally significant in the Southern Hemisphere — the southern equivalent of [[polaris|Polaris]], appearing on five national flags and in the national anthems of Australia, Brazil, and Samoa.

## Navigation

In the Southern Hemisphere, Crux serves the same function as Polaris in the north: a reliable pointer to the south celestial pole.

**Method**: project a line through the long axis of the cross (from Gacrux to Acrux) about 4.5 times the length of the cross itself — that point is approximately the south celestial pole. Cross-check using the "Pointer Stars" (Alpha and Beta Centauri, which always point toward Crux).

Unlike Polaris, there is no bright star at the south celestial pole — the closest naked-eye star is Sigma Octantis (magnitude 5.4, barely visible). Crux provides the bearing; the exact pole must be estimated.

Crux is **circumpolar** (never sets) for observers south of 34°S — southern Argentina, Chile, South Africa, southern Australia. Due to precession, the constellation will move closer to the south pole over the coming millennia.

**Precession note**: Crux was visible from southern Europe in antiquity but has drifted south since then. By AD 14,000, Crux will be visible from most of Europe.

## Five National Flags

The Southern Cross (4 or 5 stars) appears on the flags of:
- **Australia** — 5 stars (one for each state, one for territories)
- **Brazil** — 4 stars (plus others representing states)
- **New Zealand** — 4 stars
- **Papua New Guinea** — 4 stars
- **Samoa** — 5 stars

Also appears on flags of several Australian states and territories, the Mercosur trading bloc, and numerous regional flags across the southern hemisphere.

## The Coalsack — Dark Emu's Head

The Coalsack Nebula — a dark dust cloud near Acrux (Alpha Crucis) that appears as a conspicuous dark patch in the Milky Way — is the **head of the Emu in the Sky** in [[aboriginal-astronomy|Australian Aboriginal astronomy]]. Where Western astronomy draws figures from bright stars, Aboriginal Australians drew from the dark patches between stars, with the Emu's body stretching from the Coalsack through the Great Rift to Scorpius.

The "False Cross" (asterism in Carina and Vela) is slightly larger and dimmer and lacks the Pointer Stars — a classic navigation mistake for those unfamiliar with southern skies.

## Connection to the Jewel Box

Adjacent to the Coalsack is the **Jewel Box Cluster** (NGC 4755) — one of the youngest open clusters in the Milky Way (~7 million years old), containing bright blue supergiants and at least one red supergiant. Visible to the naked eye as a fuzzy star; spectacular through binoculars. Named by John Herschel for the colours visible in his telescope.
