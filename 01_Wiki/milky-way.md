# Milky Way

The barred spiral galaxy containing the Solar System — our home galaxy. The galactic environment within which the [[local-bubble]], [[interstellar-medium]], [[oort-cloud]], and all other aphelion notes exist. One of two dominant members of the Local Group alongside the Andromeda Galaxy.

| Property | Value |
|---|---|

| Dark matter mass | 1–1.5×10¹² M☉ (most of total) |

## Structure

**Central bar and bulge** — A central barred structure surrounded by a spheroidal bulge of older stars. At the very center: **Sagittarius A\*** (Sgr A*), a supermassive black hole of ~4 million M☉, confirmed by stellar orbital measurements and the Event Horizon Telescope image (2022).

**Disk** — The flat rotating plane containing most young stars, gas, and dust. Star formation is concentrated in the disk. Warped into an S-curve shape. The Sun is located ~26,000 light-years from the Galactic Center, in the **Orion-Cygnus spur** — a minor arm or spur between the Perseus and Scutum-Centaurus major arms.

**Spiral arms** — Four arms traced by gas and young stars: Norma, Scutum-Centaurus, Carina-Sagittarius, Perseus. However, infrared surveys reveal only *two* major stellar overdensities (Perseus and Scutum-Centaurus) — the Milky Way appears to have 2 major stellar arms but 4 gas arms.

**Stellar halo** — A spheroidal distribution of old stars and ~150 globular clusters surrounding the disk; 90% within 100,000 light-years of the center. ~40% of clusters orbit retrograde. No active star formation.

**Gaseous halo** — A massive hot gas halo (1–2.5 million K) extending hundreds of thousands of light-years — nearly as massive as the entire galaxy. May account for "missing baryons" in the universe.

## Solar System Location

The Solar System orbits the Galactic Center at ~220 km/s with a period of ~225–250 million years (one "galactic year"). Its current position within the [[local-bubble]] places it in a region of unusually sparse interstellar gas carved by supernovae over the past 10–20 million years.

## Formation and History

- **~13.61 billion years ago:** Milky Way began as small overdensities after the Big Bang
- **Halo formation first:** Earliest stars formed in globular clusters now comprising the stellar halo; nearly half of Milky Way matter may have come from other galaxies
- **~11 billion years ago:** Major merger with the "Kraken" galaxy
- **~10–8 billion years ago:** Disk formed as the galaxy spun up; star formation dropped by an order of magnitude during the hot-gas epoch when interstellar gas was too hot to form stars efficiently
- **Ongoing:** Currently accreting material from the Large and Small Magellanic Clouds via the Magellanic Stream

## Fate

The Milky Way sits in the "green valley" on the galaxy color-magnitude diagram — transitioning from active star formation (blue cloud) toward quiescence (red sequence). Star formation will likely extinguish within ~5 billion years. In ~4 billion years, the Milky Way will collide with the Andromeda Galaxy; the merged system will likely become an elliptical galaxy, completing the transition to the red sequence.

## Intergalactic Context

- **Local Group:** 50 closely bound galaxies dominated by the Milky Way and Andromeda
- **Virgo Supercluster:** The Local Group's parent supercluster
- **Laniakea:** The broader supercluster complex drawn toward the Great Attractor; ~500 million light-years across
