IAU Naming Conventions

The International Astronomical Union's structured system for naming solar system bodies — which embeds a deliberate mythology-astronomy mapping directly into planetary science. For trans-Neptunian objects especially, the naming rules constitute a systematic mythology encoding where orbital class determines mythological category.

This is the meta-framework that makes deliberate crossing notes (crossing-sedna, eris-dwarf-planet, and others) possible as a distinct category from accidental or observed crossings.

The Structured Mapping

Object class Naming convention Examples
Classical KBOs Creation/fertility deities from any mythology Quaoar (Tongva), Haumea (Hawaiian), Makemake (Rapa Nui)
Plutinos (2:3 Neptune resonance) Underworld deities Orcus (Etruscan underworld), Ixion (Greek underworld figure)
Scattered disc objects (similar to underworld/discord) Eris (goddess of strife)
Centaurs (crossing giant planet orbits) Centaur creatures from Greek myth Chiron, Pholus, Nessus
Jupiter Trojans (L4) Greek heroes from the Trojan War Achilles, Odysseus, Agamemnon
Jupiter Trojans (L5) Trojan heroes from the Trojan War Priam, Hector, Aeneas
Neptune Trojans Amazons Otrera
Near-Earth objects Mythological names (not creation/underworld) Apollo, Eros, Hermes

The logic: how far out an object orbits determines what kind of mythological figure it gets. The outer dark of the solar system → underworld and chthonic deities. The creative reservoir of the outer belt → creation deities. The dynamic boundary zone → liminal figures (centaurs, centaur creatures). The inner system → classical gods.

Why This Matters

The IAU system means that every time a new trans-Neptunian object is discovered and named, a deliberate mythological-astronomical correspondence is being created. The astronomers naming these objects are consciously selecting myths that resonate with the astronomical character of what they've found.

This is how sedna-dwarf-planet got Sedna (cold, dark, remote, ancient, controlling the deep), eris-dwarf-planet got Eris (her discovery caused strife), Haumea got a Hawaiian fertility goddess (she spins so fast she sheds moons — births them), and Orcus (an anti-Pluto with a nearly opposite orbit) got an Etruscan underworld deity.

General Rules

  • Names must be pronounceable, preferably one word, max 16 characters
  • Military/political leaders ineligible until 100 years after death
  • Whimsical names acceptable for ordinary asteroids (26858 Misterrogers, 274301 Wikipedia)
  • The discoverer proposes a name; the Working Group on Small Bodies Nomenclature (WGSBN) approves
  • After IAU approval, published in the WGSBN Bulletin with a citation explaining its significance

Historical Development

The naming of planets with Greek/Roman mythology names predates the IAU — it was established practice by the time Uranus (1781) and Neptune (1846) were discovered. The asteroid belt initially used female names for main-belt objects, male for unusual orbits. As the population exploded (millions of numbered objects), the mythology-per-orbital-class system became necessary to impose semantic order.

The 2006 IAU vote that defined "dwarf planet" — triggered by Eris's discovery — also clarified the naming taxonomy by defining clear orbital categories, each with its corresponding mythological domain.
