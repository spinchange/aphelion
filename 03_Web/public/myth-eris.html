Myth: Eris — Goddess of Discord

Eris is the Greek goddess of strife and discord — the force that drives competition to cruelty, rivalry to war, disagreement to catastrophe. She occupies a peculiar position in the Greek pantheon: rarely worshipped, never loved, but essential to the mythological machinery. Without Eris, the Trojan War does not begin. Without Eris, the world has no ambition, no edge, no productive struggle — and Hesiod credits her with two natures: the destructive discord of war and the useful discord of competition.

Character and Role

Eris is a daughter of Nyx (Night), sister to a cluster of dark abstractions — Death (Thanatos), Sleep (Hypnos), Doom (Moros), Fate (Keres). She appears wherever conflict breaks out and inflames it. She is close to Ares (war) and accompanies him to the battlefield, where she grows taller as battles intensify — a goddess who feeds on the conflict she causes.

In Hesiod's *Theogony*, she is simply terrible. But in *Works and Days*, Hesiod complicates her: there are two Erises. One is destructive, the sister of Ares, who causes wars and feuds. The other is the productive discord of economic competition — she makes the lazy farmer look at his rich neighbour and want to work harder. Hesiod calls the second Eris good and urges men to embrace her. This is one of the more sophisticated moral distinctions in early Greek thought: strife as value, strife as vice.

The Apple of Discord

The central Eris myth is the Judgment of Paris, which initiates the Trojan War through a causal chain she sets off with a single gesture.

Eris was not invited to the wedding of Peleus and Thetis (the sea-nymph who would become Achilles's mother). The wedding was for the gods; Eris was considered too disruptive. She came anyway and threw a golden apple inscribed *"for the fairest"* (*kallisti*) among the guests.

Three goddesses claimed it: Hera, Athena, and Aphrodite. Zeus, refusing to judge between them, delegated the decision to Paris, prince of Troy. Each goddess bribed him:

  • Hera offered power — dominion over kingdoms
  • Athena offered wisdom and skill in battle
  • Aphrodite offered the most beautiful woman in the world

Paris chose Aphrodite. The most beautiful woman was Helen, wife of Menelaus, king of Sparta. Paris went to Sparta, and either seduced or abducted Helen, triggering the decade-long Trojan War that destroyed his city and killed him.

One uninvited goddess, one thrown apple, one word — *kallisti* — and ten years of war, the fall of Troy, the Odyssey, the Aeneid. Eris does not need to do much. She just introduces the condition for conflict and lets human nature do the rest.

The Apple as Seed

The apple of discord is Eris's signature: a small, targeted injection of competitive vanity that sets irreversible processes in motion. She does not fight. She does not argue. She creates the conditions for others to destroy themselves.

This makes her mythologically distinct from Ares, who is brute force. Eris is cleverer and more economical. She is the initial condition, not the process.

The phrase "apple of discord" — an object or statement that causes strife among people — passes into English idiom from this myth and is still in active use.

Hesiod's Two Erises

The doubling in *Works and Days* is worth sitting with. Hesiod is saying that the same force — wanting what someone else has, comparing yourself to your neighbour — produces both war and agriculture, both feuds and commerce. The difference is direction and proportion.

This anticipates Adam Smith's "invisible hand" by 2,500 years: self-interest, properly channelled, produces social goods. Eris is the energy; culture is the channel.
