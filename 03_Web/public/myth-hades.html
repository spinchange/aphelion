Hades (Mythology)

The Greek god of the dead and ruler of the underworld — the third of the great Olympian brothers, last to receive his domain when the cosmos was divided by lot. His Roman name is Pluto (from *Plouton*, "the wealthy one"), which is why the dwarf planet bears his Roman name. Crossing note: crossing-pluto-hades.

The Division of the Cosmos

After Zeus led the Olympians to victory over the Titans, the three sons of Cronus divided the universe by casting lots:

  • Zeus — the sky and sovereignty over gods and men
  • Poseidon — the sea
  • Hades — the underworld, realm of the dead

The earth and Olympus were held in common by all three. But Hades's domain was the most absolute: once the dead entered, they did not leave. Hades himself rarely left — he is almost entirely absent from the active mythology of the Olympians, a remote sovereign of a realm apart.

Character and Epithets

Hades is not a god of death — that is Thanatos (Death personified). Hades simply rules over the dead once they arrive. He is neither evil nor merciful by nature: cold, implacable, and just.

People feared to speak his name. Euphemisms proliferated:

  • Plouton ("the wealthy one") — precious metals and gems come from underground; Hades owns all subterranean riches
  • Clymenus ("the renowned")
  • Eubouleus ("the good counsellor")

The Roman adoption of *Pluto* as the standard name reflects this avoidance — even in antiquity, calling the underworld god by his Greek name felt dangerous.

The Helm of Invisibility

The Cyclopes gave each of the three brothers a weapon:

  • Zeus: the thunderbolt
  • Poseidon: the trident
  • Hades: a helm of invisibility

Hades lent this helm to Perseus for his mission against Medusa, and to Athena during the Trojan War. The helm — not a weapon but a tool of concealment — suits a god who rules the unseen.

Persephone

The central myth of Hades: he abducted Persephone, daughter of Demeter (goddess of grain and harvest), and took her to the underworld as his queen. Demeter's grief caused the earth to become barren. Zeus brokered a compromise: Persephone would spend part of each year with Hades and part on the surface.

The result: the seasons. When Persephone is underground, Demeter mourns and winter comes. When Persephone returns, spring follows.

Persephone ate pomegranate seeds while in the underworld — eating the food of the dead bound her to return. The number of seeds she ate (varying accounts: three to seven) determines how many months of winter occur.

The Underworld Geography

Hades's realm is not a uniform place. It has distinct regions:

  • Elysium (Elysian Fields) — paradise for heroes and the virtuous
  • Asphodel Meadows — the grey indifferent afterlife of ordinary souls
  • Tartarus — deepest pit, punishment for the wicked and for the Titans

Key features:

  • River Styx — the oath-binding river; the gods swore unbreakable oaths by it. Souls cross it with Charon the ferryman, who requires payment (an obol coin placed on the eyes or mouth of the dead).
  • River Lethe — the river of forgetfulness; ordinary souls drank from it and forgot their past lives
  • Cerberus — the three-headed dog guarding the entrance; allowed the dead in but no one out

Mortals Who Entered

The living who descended to Hades and returned form a distinct mythological category:

  • Orpheus — entered to retrieve his wife Eurydice; his music moved Hades and Persephone to allow her return, on condition he not look back. He looked back at the threshold.
  • Heracles — captured Cerberus as his 12th labour; returned him afterward
  • Odysseus — summoned the dead (nekuia) without fully entering
  • Psyche — sent by Aphrodite to retrieve a box of beauty from Persephone
  • Sisyphus — outwitted Hades twice; eventually punished with eternal futile labour

Hades and the Olympians

Hades is sometimes listed among the twelve Olympians and sometimes excluded — his absence from Olympus (he resides permanently in his realm) made him an outsider among the gods. He had no cult to speak of in most of Greece: people did not worship him by asking for favours. They propitiated him to stay away.

His exclusion from the active divine community mirrors his domain: the world below, unseen, separate, final.
